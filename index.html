Case studies

Four projects, told plainly

Each one names the problem, the work, the result, and what Cat would change next time.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI, Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process, which created real audit risk.

What I did

Built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that requires documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus Notion workflows for New Hire and Offboarding. Held firm through early pushback that a form was too process-heavy.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

ClaudeNotionRipplingSlack

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI, 2026

Challenge

A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India, with no way for leadership to see the total cost.

What I did

Used Claude and Gemini to model severance, COBRA cost, and COBRA months owed for each person and draft the agreements, tracked on a shared spreadsheet for Payroll and IT.

Result

First-time visibility into RIF cost for leadership, and agreement prep cut from 20 minutes per employee to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

ClaudeGeminiGoogle Sheets

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell, Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

No documentation beyond outdated files, headcount in spreadsheets, and no tracked channel for employee questions.

What I did

Built a 20+ procedure SOP library, launched ASKPeople on Jira and Confluence with tiered SLAs, and made Sapling the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected through Zapier.

Result

Support for 3 to 5 countries, 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, approval cycles cut from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and 5-10 tickets a day resolved in 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

A benefits renewal with one month to spare

Ridecell

Challenge

The company was re-rated from large group to small group with only one month before renewal.

What I did

Analyzed plans and costs with the broker and switched carriers to Cigna, running a compressed open enrollment.

Result

$30K in annual savings with no disruption to employee coverage.

What I would do differently

I'd have checked the company's group-size status with the broker well before renewal, so the re-rating wouldn't have been a surprise.

30-60-90

A first six months at Planful

A proposed plan built around the responsibilities in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet the Chief People Officer, the new CEO's leadership team, and the People team to learn priorities and pain points.
  • Map the employee lifecycle, the HRIS data in Rippling, and the policies in each location Planful operates in.
  • Review open employee relations matters and current employee feedback.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Run a data and policy audit to find gaps in HRIS integrity, reporting, and compliance.
  • Agree the people priorities with the CPO and turn them into clear, practical plans.
  • Choose one lifecycle process to simplify, using an approval-and-audit-trail pattern.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Publish the first monthly People report for the People team and business leaders.
  • Launch one program with leaders, such as manager enablement or the next performance cycle.
  • Pilot an AI workflow that removes manual work from the team, with clear owners.
By month 6

The outcome

  • A People function that runs reliably: clean HRIS data, current policies, and documented core processes.
  • A monthly reporting rhythm that leaders read and act on.
  • A small People team with clear priorities, and leaders who know where to bring the sensitive matters.

This is a starting point. Cat would adjust it after the first conversations with the CPO and company leaders.
